# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **Wingpeople** (primary, iOS app): social connectors like Lia who keep a roster of single friends. Context: at a house party or bar, phone in hand, mid-conversation — they need to find and show a friend's profile in seconds, one-handed, in dim light.
- **Party guests** (web, no account): someone the wingperson just met. They receive a profile link on their own phone. Context: 30 seconds of attention, zero patience for friction, deciding whether to say hi.
- **The single friends** (web, no account): receive a chat link by text. Context: surprised, curious, slightly nervous. The interface must make them feel vouched-for and in control (STOP always works).

## Product Purpose

Matchbook turns wingpeople into great matchmakers: log your single friends as beautiful profiles, show them at parties, share a link, and let two people chat account-free with a built-in escape hatch (either side types STOP and the chat locks forever). Success = intros that feel human and safe, not app-mediated.

## Brand Personality

Flirty, confident, vouched-for. The voice of your funniest friend making an introduction — a wink, not a swipe. Warm but grown-up. Copy is playful ("Your single friends deserve better PR"), never crude, never desperate.

## Anti-references

- **Tinder/Hinge and swipe culture**: no flame gradients, no hot-or-not mechanics, no neon dating-app kitsch. Matchbook is introductions between real friends, not a meat market.
- **Cream/terracotta warm-neutral default** (that's Dear Date's family; Matchbook has its own identity — and it's the saturated AI default anyway).
- Corporate SaaS sterility; generic startup gradients.

## Design Principles

1. **The friend is the hero.** Photos and the wingperson's pitch carry every screen; the UI frames, never competes.
2. **Party-proof.** Big targets, high contrast, one-handed reach, legible in dim light and bright sun. Every core action ≤2 taps.
3. **Vouched, not marketed.** The human vouching (the wingperson's name, their words) is the trust signal — surface it everywhere it matters.
4. **Safety is a feature, not fine print.** STOP and "Not interested" are visible, confident affordances, styled with the same care as "Say hi."
5. **A wink, not a shout.** Playfulness lives in copy, one lipstick-red brand color, and small motion moments — not in clutter or gimmicks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, touch targets ≥44px, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all animation. Web pages must work on any phone browser (guests/friends can't choose their device).
